About

I write the bet down before I design it.

Senior Product Designer working across onboarding, payments and operational tooling. Most of my work starts as a written hypothesis with success criteria attached, so that when it ships there's a way to tell whether it worked rather than whether it looked good.

On Booking.com for Business that meant fixing what we could inside a legacy system before committing to a full redesign, testing each change to confirm it actually worked rather than guessing. On Unleashed's dashboard it meant descoping an operational data dump down to what teams actually needed to make faster decisions.

I work closely with research, engineering and product throughout a project, not after the fact, because the insights that matter usually surface in that overlap.

How I work

01

State the hypothesis

A value hypothesis with numbers attached before any UI exists. It's what makes a result readable afterwards — and what makes descoping an argument rather than a preference.

02

Fix before rebuild

Cheap changes inside the existing system first, tested. If clearer error copy already moves the number, that's evidence for the bigger investment, not a substitute for it.

03

Test at higher fidelity, sooner

Twenty-plus moderated sessions on B4B, and AI-built prototypes so participants react to something that behaves like the product instead of a wireframe.

04

Design for the market, not the template

Japan reads minimalism as evasive; Australia reads BNPL as budgeting. The research decides the density, not a house style.
