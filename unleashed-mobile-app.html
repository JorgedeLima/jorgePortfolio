Context & challenge

Inventory and operational users needed real-time visibility outside the office. The existing mobile experience lacked both parity and clarity — and the instinct in the room was to close the parity gap.

The goal wasn't feature parity — it was contextual mobility.

Role & contribution

  • Defined the mobile-first prioritisation framework
  • Led the experience simplification strategy
  • Coordinated with backend teams on data optimisation
  • Oversaw interaction design and mobile UI system alignment

Research & insights

  1. 01Users opened mobile primarily for quick checks, not sustained work.
  2. 02Complex workflows were rarely completed on a phone at all.
  3. 03Speed and clarity outweighed feature breadth every time.

Goals & strategic objectives

  • Enable high-frequency operational tasks
  • Reduce friction in mobile interactions
  • Improve responsiveness and perceived speed

Design strategy & approach

  • Contextual prioritisation — what matters when you are standing in a warehouse
  • Streamlined task flows
  • Simplified navigation architecture
  • Clear touch interaction affordances

Solution

  • Redesigned core mobile flows
  • Reduced step count in key actions
  • Improved search and quick-access functions
  • Aligned UI with the desktop ecosystem for continuity

Results & impact

  • Increased mobile engagement
  • Improved task completion efficiency
  • Strengthened ecosystem coherence

Reflection & learnings

Mobile isn't a smaller desktop — it's a different intent space.

This project sharpened how the team defines feature priority: by context rather than parity. Future potential is offline-first capability for warehouse environments, where connectivity is the real constraint.
